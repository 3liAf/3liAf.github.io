Why Your RAG Pipeline is Missing Context: Debugging Sentence Fractures

By Ali Afzal Yousuf · November 2026 · 5 min read

Most engineering teams building Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) treat document chunking as an afterthought. They configure an arbitrary chunk size (e.g. 500 characters), add a 10% overlap, push the embeddings to Pinecone or Qdrant, and wonder why retrieval degrades on real-world queries.

When dense vector search fails to pull the correct context, the culprit is rarely the embedding model. In production, the most frequent failure mode is sentence fracturing.

1. What is a Sentence Fracture?

Dense vector embeddings encode syntactic and semantic dependencies between clauses in high-dimensional space. When an arbitrary splitter cleaves a paragraph mid-sentence based on a hard character or token limit, two critical failures occur:

  • Context Severance: The first half of the sentence loses its subject or qualification; the second half loses its predicate.
  • Semantic Dilution: The embedding vector for the fractured fragment represents grammatical noise, resulting in poor cosine similarity scores during top-k retrieval.
Example Fracture:
Original: "Because of the strict compliance regulations enacted in Q3, all financial audit logs must be retained for 7 years."

Chunk A: "...because of the strict compliance regulations enacted in Q3," (No retention rule!)
Chunk B: "all financial audit logs must be retained for 7 years." (No context as to why or when!)

2. The Overlap Dilemma

To mask fractures, developers routinely ramp up chunk_overlap to 25% or 30%. But excessive overlap introduces severe downstream costs:

  • Context Window Waste: Duplicating tokens across chunks fills the LLM context window with redundant text rather than diverse evidence.
  • Index Cost Multipliers: You pay embedding providers to embed the exact same tokens two or three times.

4. Measuring Retrieval Accuracy with rag-eval-metrics

To verify whether chunk tuning improves results, evaluate your retriever with zero-dependency Information Retrieval metrics:

pip install git+https://github.com/3liAf/rag-eval-metrics.git
from rageval import hit_rate_at_k, mean_reciprocal_rank, ndcg_at_k

retrieved = ["chunk_84", "chunk_12", "chunk_99"]
ground_truth = {"chunk_12"}

print("Hit Rate @ 3:", hit_rate_at_k(retrieved, ground_truth, k=3))
print("MRR:         ", mean_reciprocal_rank([[retrieved, ground_truth]]))
print("NDCG @ 3:    ", ndcg_at_k(retrieved, ground_truth, k=3))

Summary

  1. Never split purely on raw character counts. Use recursive, sentence-aware boundary splitters.
  2. Audit document boundaries with the RAG Visualizer before running large embedding batches.
  3. Track Hit Rate @ 5 and NDCG @ 5 to ensure your chunking adjustments actually improve retrieval accuracy.
